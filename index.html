Chapter One
Why your petrol price is set in Washington

Before we get to Iran, before we get to Gaza, before we get to any of the wars you are watching, you need to understand one thing. It is not complicated. But once you see it, you cannot unsee it.

Imagine a moneylender in your neighbourhood. He has lent out ten times more money than he actually has. Those loans earn him interest. That interest keeps the whole thing alive. The moment people stop taking new loans, or stop paying back old ones, the entire structure collapses. So the moneylender does not stop. He cannot stop. He finds new borrowers. He pressures existing ones. He does whatever it takes to keep the money moving.

Now make that moneylender the entire Western financial system. And make those borrowers entire countries.

What global finance claims to own
$300T
Every stock, bond, loan, derivative, and paper claim in existence. What the financial world says it is owed.
What the world actually produces in a year
$110T
Every meal, car, service, and piece of software actually made. The real economy.

The gap is $195 trillion. Three times more money has been promised than exists. To stay alive, that system must constantly find new places to invest, new markets to enter, new assets to absorb. It is not greed. It is physics. A shark that stops moving dies.

This one sentence explains seventy years of foreign policy
When a country opens its resources to global capital, we call it development. When it refuses, we call it a threat to stability. And then we destabilize it.

Wars, sanctions, regime changes. Not mistakes. Not failures. Not the work of evil individuals. They are the maintenance operations of a system that cannot survive without constant expansion. Understanding this does not make you a conspiracy theorist. It makes you literate.

Chapter Two
The four steps they always follow

Say you are a small country. You have oil. Or lithium. Or a hundred million young people who represent a massive consumer market. You elect a leader who says: we will manage our own resources. We will industrialize ourselves. We will not hand our national assets to foreign companies.

Here is exactly what happens next.

1
Sanctions: turn the economy into a slow suffocation
Your country is cut off from the international banking system. You cannot receive payment for your exports. You cannot pay for imports. Medicine becomes scarce. Fuel becomes expensive. Inflation destroys savings overnight. Your middle class is wiped out first. People stop blaming the foreign power that imposed the sanctions and start blaming their own government. Eventually something gives. Right now this is happening simultaneously to Iran, Venezuela, Cuba, North Korea, Russia, and Myanmar.
2
Debt: offer a lifeline with conditions attached
A financial crisis arrives. It may have been engineered; it may be natural. Either way, the IMF appears with a loan. The loan has conditions: privatize your state companies, end fuel and food subsidies, remove protections for local industry, open your financial system to foreign capital. You have no choice but to accept. Local businesses collapse under the competition. Poverty rises. You need another loan to service the first one. Pakistan has signed IMF agreements 24 times. Each time the conditions tighten.
3
Regime change: remove the government without invading
Fund opposition groups. Place stories in foreign-language media. Identify generals or bureaucrats who can be turned. Organize protests at the right moment. If the leader is too popular, manufacture a scandal. The US government has officially acknowledged doing this in Iran in 1953, Guatemala in 1954, and Chile in 1973. Not rumours. Confirmed. In official archives. Declassified. These are three countries in which an elected government was removed and replaced with one that cooperated.
4
War: destroy the country and then profit from rebuilding it
The last resort. Also, for specific industries, the most profitable option. The country is bombed. Infrastructure is destroyed. Then the reconstruction contracts are awarded to companies from the countries that did the bombing. After Iraq, Halliburton alone received $39.5 billion in contracts. After Libya, oil fields were operational within months of Gaddafi's death. The rubble is not a tragedy to be managed. It is a market to be entered.
"I spent 33 years in the Marines. I was a gangster for capitalism. I helped make Mexico safe for American oil interests. I helped make Haiti and Cuba a decent place for the National City Bank boys."
General Smedley Butler, the most decorated US Marine in history, in his 1935 book War is a Racket. He ran this playbook personally for three decades, then spent the rest of his life describing it.
Chapter Three
Five countries. One pattern.

If you are skeptical, good. Skepticism is the right response. So do not take any of this on faith. Instead, look at what each country on this list did just before it was targeted. Look at what happened to its resources afterward. Then decide for yourself.

1951 — Iran
Prime Minister Mosaddegh nationalized Iranian oil
He was democratically elected. He said the oil under Iranian soil should belong to Iranians, not the Anglo-Persian Oil Company. Britain and the US called him a communist threat. In 1953, a joint CIA and MI6 operation removed him from power. He spent the rest of his life under house arrest. Iranian oil was handed back to British and American companies. The CIA officially confirmed the coup in 2013.
2000 — Iraq
Saddam Hussein switched Iraqi oil sales from dollars to euros
In November 2000, Iraq began accepting euros for oil. In March 2003, the US invaded. No weapons of mass destruction were found. Not one. The documented civilian death toll: 150,000. Conservative estimates of total deaths, including those from destroyed hospitals and water systems: over 500,000. Oil sales returned to dollars. Halliburton received $39.5 billion in contracts. A leaked British government memo confirmed the invasion decision was made in advance and the intelligence shaped around it.
2009 — Libya
Gaddafi proposed a gold-backed African currency for oil trade
A single pan-African currency backed by gold would have allowed African nations to trade oil without using dollars. Hillary Clinton's emails, released through FOIA, show this currency plan was a primary concern. In 2011, NATO launched airstrikes under a humanitarian justification. Gaddafi was killed. Libyan oil fields were operational within months. By 2017, CNN was filming open slave auctions in the country NATO had liberated.
2022 — Russia
Russia demanded payment for gas in rubles and yuan, bypassing the dollar
Within weeks, Russia was cut from SWIFT, the backbone of global banking. $300 billion in Russian sovereign reserves held in Western banks were frozen. This was unprecedented: the seizure of a nation's savings held abroad. The stated justification was Ukraine. The financial action specifically targeted the currency switch that predated the invasion.
2026 — Iran, as you read this
Iran had been pricing oil in non-dollar currencies since 2023
On February 28, 2026, US and Israeli forces launched coordinated strikes. Iran's Supreme Leader was killed in the opening hours. More than 1,230 Iranians have died as of this writing. The nuclear justification was prepared and ready. The financial motive had been building for seventy years.

Five countries. Five different official reasons given to the public. Five different villains of the week. One thing each country did just before it was targeted: it moved its oil transactions away from the dollar. If this happened twice, it would be coincidence. It has now happened five times in seventy years with no exceptions. At some point coincidence stops being an explanation.

Chapter Four
Iran is not the prize. Iran is the door.

People are dying right now. Specific human beings in specific cities. And the official reason being given on every major news channel is about nuclear weapons and regional security. Here is the geography that nobody on television is showing you.

The official justification
Iran was weeks away from a nuclear bomb. Israel had no choice. The US is maintaining regional stability and protecting its allies.
What the geography actually shows
Iran is the only land corridor to Central Asia that operates outside the dollar system. Remove Iran as an independent actor and an entire region worth over $5 trillion becomes accessible.

Behind Iran lies a region that almost nobody in Indian media discusses. It is landlocked, it is resource-rich beyond imagination, and its only practical southern exit runs directly through Iranian territory.

The resources behind the door Iran was guarding
Kazakhstan: oil and uranium
$2T+
3% of the world's oil and 43% of the world's uranium. The only southern entry point not controlled by Russia or China ran through Iran.
Turkmenistan: natural gas
$1T+
The 4th largest natural gas reserves on earth. Completely landlocked. The only southern corridor to world markets ran through Iran.
Lithium and rare earths across the region
$2T+
The materials inside every electric vehicle and solar panel. Without them the clean energy transition stops. The southern corridor to access them ran through Iran.
The democratic deficit nobody talks about
Only 25% of Americans support this war. The US Senate voted 53 to 47 to continue it without a full Congressional authorization debate. The public is against it. Their elected representatives were bypassed. Over a thousand people have died. So who made this decision? And can you name them?

Now watch for the following, because it happens every time without exception. Within 12 to 18 months of any ceasefire, major energy companies will file for access to Iranian oil assets. A reconstruction fund will be announced, managed by a Western financial institution. Iranian oil will return to dollar-denominated pricing. The people who were bombed will then pay, with interest, for the infrastructure used to bomb them. It happened in Iraq. In Libya. Watch for it in Iran.

Chapter Five
You are not watching this. You are inside it.

If you have read this far and thought: this is about distant countries, terrible but not my problem, then this chapter is specifically for you. Every dynamic described in this piece is actively running inside India right now. On your savings. On your fuel price. On your government's foreign policy choices. On this country's future.

🏦
Your family's savings are the target
BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager, with $10 trillion under management, has partnered with Jio Financial Services to enter Indian households. Indians hold an estimated $3 to $4 trillion in gold, fixed deposits, and savings. The strategy is to route that wealth into global financial markets through a brand Indians already trust. The extraction does not require a warship. It requires a good app and a familiar name.
🛣️
India's best strategic move in decades just died
India spent years building the Chabahar port in Iran to access Central Asia by bypassing Pakistan. It was India's one independent route to $5 trillion in untapped resources. It would have changed India's strategic position permanently. The Iran war ended that calculation in the first week of strikes. India's Central Asia strategy is now paused indefinitely.
🔇
The silence that told you everything
A US submarine sank an Iranian warship in the Indian Ocean, India's own strategic backyard. India issued no statement. Not a whisper of protest. Because Israel is India's primary supplier of drones and precision missiles. The silence was not neutrality or diplomacy. It was the price of the arms contract, paid in the currency of looking the other way when people die in your neighbourhood.

A fortnight from an energy crisis
India imports 85% of its oil. If the Strait of Hormuz closes for even two weeks, there is no ready alternative supply chain. Petrol prices spike within days. Trucking costs rise. Food prices follow. Inflation climbs. The rupee weakens. A conflict India had no vote in, fought for reasons India was not consulted about, can bring Indian cities to their knees within a fortnight.

India's current leadership made a deliberate choice to build these dependencies: the arms relationship with Israel, the financial opening to BlackRock, the silence on American military activity in Indian waters. The people who will live with the consequences of those choices are not the ones who made them.

Chapter Six
They described it themselves

You should not have to take any of this on faith. So do not. Here is what the people who designed and ran this system said about it directly. In public. Under their own names. On record. These are not leaked to a conspiracy website. They are in government archives, television broadcasts, and official policy documents.

"In the councils of government, we must guard against the acquisition of unwarranted influence, whether sought or unsought, by the military-industrial complex. The potential for the disastrous rise of misplaced power exists and will persist."
Dwight D. Eisenhower, farewell address as US President, January 17, 1961. Eisenhower had commanded Allied forces in World War II and spent eight years as president. This is what he chose to say on his last day in office. He was warning about what was being built inside his own country.
"The intelligence and facts were being fixed around the policy."
The Downing Street Memo, a classified British Cabinet briefing dated July 23, 2002 — eight months before the Iraq invasion. The decision to invade had already been made. The intelligence about weapons of mass destruction was being manufactured to justify it retroactively. This document is real, uncontested, and freely available online. None of it was denied by the British government when it leaked.
"Further, the process of transformation, even if it brings revolutionary change, is likely to be a long one, absent some catastrophic and catalyzing event, like a new Pearl Harbor."
Project for the New American Century, September 2000. A policy document calling explicitly for US military dominance and regime change across the Middle East. Signed by Dick Cheney, Donald Rumsfeld, and Paul Wolfowitz. Published exactly twelve months before September 11, 2001. All three men then ran the wars that followed. The document is freely available online.
"We came, we saw, he died." [laughing]
Hillary Clinton, US Secretary of State, CBS News, October 20, 2011. She was reacting to news of Muammar Gaddafi's death. Six weeks before the NATO intervention, Gaddafi had formally proposed a gold-backed pan-African currency for oil trade. Clinton was in a television interview, discussing the killing of a head of state, and she was laughing.

These are the architects. Not anonymous sources. Not dissidents. The people who held the offices, signed the orders, and gave the briefings. The question worth sitting with is not whether this is real. It is why nobody ever placed these quotes in front of you in this order before now.

Chapter Seven
The question Krishnamurti kept asking

Jiddu Krishnamurti was born in Madanapalle, Andhra Pradesh in 1895. He became one of the most important philosophers of the twentieth century. He spent his entire life asking one kind of question. Not about politics or economics. About the mind doing the observing.

He believed that most human suffering, including war, was not caused by evil people in power. It was caused by ordinary people who had never learned to look at reality without the filter of what they had been told to believe. He thought the inability to see clearly was the root of almost everything.

Three questions from Krishnamurti worth sitting with today
"Why do you accept the world as it is? Why do you not revolt? Not violently — but deeply, inwardly, so that you begin to understand it?"
"Can you look at the fact of what is happening in the world without any conclusion, without any ideology, without any system of thought guiding what you see?"
"The moment you follow someone, you cease to follow truth."
Jiddu Krishnamurti, 1895 to 1986. Born in Madanapalle, Andhra Pradesh.

The system described in this piece does not fear outrage. Outrage is easy to redirect. It does not fear protesters. Protesters can be managed. What it cannot survive is a critical mass of people who simply see it clearly and refuse to be confused about what they are watching.

That is why the news cycle moves so fast. That is why there is always a new villain, a new emergency, a new thing to be angry about. Not by accident. By design. Confusion is the product. Your attention is the raw material.

1
Every time a war starts, ask one question
Who profits from the outcome? Not from the stated reason. From what actually happens afterward. Who gets the reconstruction contracts? Whose share price rises in the first week of fighting? Which territories, ports, and resources become accessible when it ends? Follow that trail. The real reason shows up within months.
2
Learn to separate three things media constantly blurs
Documented fact: the CIA coup in Iran in 1953 is in official archives. Reasonable inference: Western capital will enter post-war Iran because that is what happened in every comparable case. Speculation: specific timelines and individuals. Most journalism and most propaganda blend these three. Once you learn to separate them, almost everything becomes easier to evaluate.
3
Go to the primary sources yourself
The Downing Street Memo is a Google search away. The CIA's official acknowledgment of the 1953 Iran coup is on the CIA's own website. The PNAC document is freely available. The most important evidence is not hidden behind paywalls or classified vaults. It is sitting in plain sight, waiting for someone to read it in the right order.
4
Share clarity, not outrage
Outrage spreads but does not teach. A clear explanation, passed from one person to another, is harder to dismiss and harder to redirect. This system has survived every angry protest movement in living memory. It has not yet survived being understood by enough people simultaneously. That gap is still open.
